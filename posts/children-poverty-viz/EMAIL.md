## 5. Body, part two

```
Now, today we are not going to address the elephant in the room – that the official poverty measure that is used to determine the definition of poverty is still based on a framework designed in 1963. Nope, not going to mention that at all… But if you missed it, you can read our coverage of that here: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/frozen-in-1963/

For augment's sake, let's just take the Census's word on this. The poverty rate is in decline for America.

Here's the problem with that. I don't know one American who lives in America. I know people who live in downtown Dayton, the Upper West Side of Manhattan, Silicon Valley, Springfield, and the outskirts of Nashville. And each of them experiences completely different versions of "America."

Look, if our goal is to use poverty data as a talking point, the national level data works fine for that. But if our goal is to really understand poverty in America, we need to do better. We need to study poverty across this country and seek to understand why there such extreme outliers. We need to ask whether it's appropriate to celebrate the poverty data when we still have 2,385 school districts and 7.9 million children living in poverty across this country. This map – a complete rebuild of a visualization that we originally published in June 2026 – helps you see the daunting problem still facing America's children.

Poverty is just another example of a problem that cannot properly be boiled down into one statistic. The story any one statistic tells is very different from the one a data visualization can tell.
```
